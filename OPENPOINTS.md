| N-17 | **Linear algebra moved to MathemaSharp.LinearAlgebra (2026-11-06).** The routines behind `numpy.linalg` (LU with partial pivoting and its solve, sign and log-determinant, cyclic-Jacobi symmetric eigendecomposition, one-sided-Jacobi SVD, basis completion, and the general/complex eigenproblem: balancing, Hessenberg, shifted QR) are now `DenseLinearAlgebra` and `GeneralEigen` in `MathemaSharp.LinearAlgebra`, on flat row-major `double[]`, with no array type. `np.Inv/Solve/SlogDet/Det/Eigh/EigvalsH/Svd/SingularValues/MatrixRank/LstSq/Pinv/Eig/Eigvals` keep their API and call them; what stays in NDSharp is the NDArray plumbing (stacks, broadcasting, dtype, errors as `NDLinAlgException`), the numpy sign convention applied to the leading singular triplet, and `LstSq`/`Pinv` built on `Svd`. Not done: generic `T` versions of these routines (they work in double, as numpy.linalg here always did), and a second look at the Jacobi methods against LAPACK for speed on large matrices. | done |

| N-14 | **MathemaSharp as the numeric engine (2026-11-06).** NDSharp references `MathemaSharp.Kernels` (BCL only). On it, for every real dtype including float16: `sum prod max min mean var std cumsum cumprod any all count_nonzero argmax argmin diff median percentile quantile` (all 13 numpy `method=` values), `sort`, `argsort` (stable, NaN last), `unique` (through argsort) and `searchsorted` (new; both sides, `sorter`) for every real dtype including bool and float16 and for complex numbers (NumPy's lexicographic order, defined in the kernels: .NET 11 gives `Complex` the arithmetic of generic math but no ordering), plus new `partition`/`argpartition` (a full sort satisfies numpy's guarantee; the order of the other elements differs from numpy's introselect, which does not specify it) and `lexsort` (stable passes from the first key to the last), `matmul`/`dot` (all real and complex dtypes, any batch and layout), `cov` and `corrcoef`, `gradient`, `trapezoid`/`trapz` (new), `histogram` (new: integer or explicit bins, `range`, `density`; no weights, no bin-rule strings) and the whole `nan*` family (new: `nansum nanprod nanmean nanvar nanstd nanmax nanmin nanmedian nanpercentile nanquantile nanargmax nanargmin nancumsum nancumprod`; complex is refused). The general engine that used to fold arrays (`Red` in `Reduce.cs`, about 570 lines with the old loops of matmul, sort, median, percentile, gradient and cov) was **removed on 2026-11-06**; NDSharp now has one implementation of these functions, the MathemaSharp kernels. It is recoverable from the git tag `engine-before-removal-20261106`. Complex input is refused (explicit `NDNotSupportedException`) by prod, max, min, var, argmax, cumsum, cumprod, median, percentile, cov and corrcoef; complex sum and mean go through their real and imaginary parts, complex sort/argsort/unique/searchsorted run on the kernels. Tests: the results of 8,964 cases (`Fixtures/kernelpath-golden.txt`) are fingerprints produced by the former engine, in a run where every kernel result was also required to agree with it element by element; the nan* functions are checked against numpy's formulas written with elementary operations, partition/lexsort against their definitions. New cases need a hand check first (`NDSHARP_GOLDEN=add`, see `Golden.cs`). The FFT (`Fft.cs`, 281 to 158 lines) now calls `MathemaSharp.Kernels.Fourier` for the transform of a line, the Hermitian completion, the half-spectrum inverse, the scales and the frequencies; `FftTests` fix 584 results produced by the previous in-NDSharp implementation (all dtypes, lengths 1 to 255, axes, norms, padding, cropping, n-D). float16 is computed in float16 arithmetic as the engine did (numpy accumulates in float32: not verified here). Not compared with real numpy (not installed here). Changed edge cases: gradient with data and positions of different dtypes now processes both in their common dtype (numpy computes the positions in their own dtype first), and the result is returned in the data's dtype. Open: `ptp` (still `max - min` on the kernels), `unique` over axes (missing), complex sums/products/cumulative functions (done through real and imaginary parts by the engine; numpy pairs the accumulators differently, so the last bits can differ), `argsort`, `unique`, `bincount`, `searchsorted`/`partition` (missing), the rest of linear algebra (`det`, `inv`, `solve`, `lstsq`, `pinv`, `svd`, `eig`), the elementwise functions (their scalar math is already .NET generic math, `T.Sin` and so on, the same primitives `MathemaSharp.Core.GenericMath` forwards; what is NDSharp-specific are the numpy rules for `floor_divide`, `mod`, integer `power`, `sign` and `rint`, to be moved to `GenericMath` and shared with MatSharp after a comparison with Octave), `inner`/`outer`/`cross`/`convolve` still run only in NDSharp. The product kernel adds left to right in the type's own arithmetic (as the engine did); NumPy uses BLAS, whose grouping differs, so the last bits can differ from real NumPy (not compared here); pack `MathemaSharp.Kernels` into the feed before NDSharp is packed (NDSharp now depends on it); a public signature change broke the prebuilt `NDSharp.Frame` once (MissingMethodException): keep old signatures as overloads. | partly done |
